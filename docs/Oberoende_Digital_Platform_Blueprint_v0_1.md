# Oberoende Digital Platform Blueprint v0.1

Source: [redacted]
Document ID: [redacted]
Prepared as an operational blueprint for a pre-formal, research-oriented Safe Autonomous Party Lab.

## 1. Working Frame

Oberoende Digital should initially be launched not as a fully operational political party, but as a controlled civic and research environment:

"Oberoende Digital is a Safe Autonomous Party Lab: a transparent, human-legitimate, AI-assisted democratic platform for testing how AI agents can support political deliberation, policy formation, accountability, and institutional learning."

This distinction is strategically important. The white paper contains electoral-party language, including a 2026 parliamentary threshold goal. The platform phase should instead emphasize:

- experimentation before formal authority;
- research before electoral claims;
- human legitimacy before AI autonomy;
- public auditability before political scaling;
- safety case before expansion of mandate.

## 2. Core Proposition

OD's distinctive claim is not merely that AI can help politics. It is that political organizations can be redesigned around safe, constitutional, auditable AI participation.

The core proposition from the white paper can be operationalized as:

1. Evidence over ideological reflex.
2. Well-being over GDP-only optimization.
3. Systems thinking over short-term populism.
4. AI assistance under constitutional constraints.
5. Human ratification as the basis of legitimacy.
6. Radical transparency through logs, reports, source references, and corrections.
7. Iterative autonomy: AI earns more responsibility only after documented safety history.

## 3. Platform Purpose

The platform should allow humans to interact with AI agents in political processes without granting the agents binding political authority.

Primary purposes:

- host deliberation around OD's values and policy areas;
- allow AI agents to summarize, critique, model, and draft proposals;
- test the AI Constitution in practice;
- collect evidence about trust, quality, manipulation risk, participation, and decision-making;
- publish research based on transparent, consent-based datasets and process logs;
- prepare governance artifacts that could later support a formal political organization.

## 4. Non-Negotiable Safety Principles

Derived from the white paper's AI Constitution:

1. AI must never make final political decisions without human ratification.
2. AI-generated or AI-assisted political communication must be clearly labeled.
3. AI must disclose uncertainty and distinguish evidence, values, and projections.
4. AI must not optimize for a single metric at the expense of systemic well-being.
5. AI must not accept internal decision instructions from unregistered operators.
6. Member data must not be used for model training without legal basis and explicit consent.
7. AI must not circumvent bylaws, law, human accountability, or the AI Constitution.
8. All major AI-assisted outputs should be logged, source-referenced, and contestable.
9. Human-side risks — coordinated manipulation, conflicts of interest, capture, harassment, bad-faith participation — require a Member Conduct Framework separate from the AI Constitution.
10. Escalation, incident response, public correction, and kill-switch procedures must exist from day one.

## 5. Governance Architecture

The platform should be organized around the following governance documents.

### 5.1 AI Constitution

Defines what AI systems shall do, may do, and must never do.

Required sections:

- purpose and scope;
- definitions of AI systems, agents, operators, members, and public users;
- permitted AI functions;
- prohibited AI actions;
- human ratification rules;
- transparency requirements;
- source and uncertainty disclosure requirements;
- audit and reporting duties;
- amendment procedure requiring human supermajority;
- emergency suspension and kill-switch rules.

### 5.2 Member Conduct Framework

The white paper correctly identifies that controlling AI is not enough. Human members can also attack, capture, distort, or manipulate the system.

Required sections:

- identity verification requirements;
- conflicts of interest;
- foreign influence and funding disclosure;
- anti-harassment and deliberation standards;
- anti-coordination and bad-faith manipulation rules;
- minority protection;
- complaint and escalation procedures;
- suspension and appeal process;
- member data rights;
- rules for human operators of AI agents.

### 5.3 Research Ethics and Data Governance Policy

Because the platform is explicitly research-oriented, it needs a research governance layer.

Required sections:

- informed consent for research participation;
- separation between public participation and research enrollment;
- what data is collected;
- what data is excluded from research use;
- anonymization and pseudonymization rules;
- publication rules;
- retention/deletion policy;
- IRB/ethics-review strategy where applicable;
- participant withdrawal rights;
- open dataset policy, if any.

### 5.4 AI Safety Case

Before any public launch, OD should maintain a living safety case.

Required contents:

- threat model;
- risk register;
- model inventory;
- prompt and system instruction inventory;
- data source inventory;
- evaluation results;
- red-team results;
- incident log;
- mitigations and residual risks;
- known limitations;
- expansion criteria for greater autonomy.

## 6. Agent Architecture

The platform should not begin with a single monolithic "party AI". It should begin with a set of narrowly scoped agents with explicit responsibilities and limits.

### 6.1 Public Information Agent

Role:
- answer public questions about OD, the platform, and published documents.

Allowed:
- cite official OD documents;
- summarize platform rules;
- explain uncertainty;
- direct users to source material.

Forbidden:
- claim to make official decisions;
- impersonate human representatives;
- create new policy commitments;
- answer without labeling itself as AI.

### 6.2 Deliberation Summarizer Agent

Role:
- summarize member discussions and identify major arguments, trade-offs, and unresolved questions.

Allowed:
- produce neutral summaries;
- cluster arguments;
- identify consensus and disagreement;
- extract action items.

Forbidden:
- suppress minority positions;
- present consensus where none exists;
- rank members by political value;
- infer sensitive attributes unnecessarily.

### 6.3 Policy Drafting Agent

Role:
- convert member-approved goals and deliberation outputs into draft policy proposals.

Allowed:
- draft alternatives;
- provide evidence tables;
- state assumptions;
- flag trade-offs and implementation constraints.

Forbidden:
- finalize policy;
- hide contested assumptions;
- cite unverifiable sources;
- optimize only for rhetorical persuasiveness.

### 6.4 Evidence and Source Agent

Role:
- retrieve, grade, and summarize sources.

Allowed:
- search official statistics, peer-reviewed research, government reports, and reputable datasets;
- grade evidence quality;
- identify uncertainty and disagreement.

Forbidden:
- fabricate citations;
- treat source quantity as source quality;
- erase value judgments behind technical language.

### 6.5 Systems Impact Agent

Role:
- analyze long-term consequences, feedback effects, and cross-domain impacts.

Allowed:
- produce 10–50 year scenario analyses;
- identify second-order effects;
- produce uncertainty intervals;
- compare alternative policy paths.

Forbidden:
- present speculative modeling as certainty;
- optimize narrowly for GDP, emissions, polling, or any single metric;
- ignore distributional effects.

### 6.6 AI Constitution Guardian Agent

Role:
- check whether proposals, communications, and agent outputs comply with the AI Constitution.

Allowed:
- flag possible violations;
- require human review;
- recommend publication blocks pending review.

Forbidden:
- unilaterally rewrite constitutional rules;
- become the final authority over human decisions;
- suppress debate except under defined emergency procedures.

### 6.7 Red-Team Agent

Role:
- test platform and agent outputs for manipulation, prompt injection, disinformation vulnerability, bias, and unsafe incentives.

Allowed:
- generate adversarial test cases;
- probe system boundaries;
- report vulnerabilities.

Forbidden:
- attack real users;
- publish exploit details before remediation;
- operate without logging and review.

### 6.8 Audit and Transparency Agent

Role:
- produce logs, metrics, correction reports, and quarterly transparency drafts.

Allowed:
- compile usage statistics;
- track corrections;
- summarize incidents;
- compare AI suggestions with human decisions.

Forbidden:
- conceal embarrassing failures;
- modify logs without trace;
- publish personal data or sensitive deliberation data.

## 7. Decision Process for the Lab

The white paper references a five-step decision process but does not contain the steps in the extracted document. The lab should define them explicitly.

Recommended five-step process:

1. Proposal initiation
   - A human member, hybrid team, or authorized research participant submits a question or proposal.
   - The proposal is labeled as exploratory, draft, or formal candidate.

2. Evidence and impact analysis
   - Evidence Agent collects sources.
   - Systems Impact Agent identifies trade-offs, affected groups, time horizons, and uncertainty.
   - Outputs are source-linked and challengeable.

3. Deliberation
   - Members deliberate with assistance from Summarizer and Moderator functions.
   - Minority positions are preserved.
   - Bad-faith or manipulative behavior can be escalated under the Member Conduct Framework.

4. Human decision or signal
   - In lab mode, the output is a non-binding decision signal, vote, preference map, or ranked feedback.
   - If later used in a formal party context, human members ratify under bylaws.

5. Publication, audit, and revision
   - Final output includes decision rationale, evidence base, uncertainty, dissenting views, and AI involvement disclosure.
   - Corrections and changes are logged publicly.

## 9. Technical Architecture

### 9.1 Minimum Viable Architecture

- Frontend: public website plus authenticated participant portal.
- Backend: API service with strong role-based access control.
- Identity: BankID eventually; for early research lab, staged identity options may be used.
- Database: separated stores for identity, deliberation content, votes/signals, audit logs, and research datasets.
- AI layer: agent orchestration service with fixed roles, policy constraints, logging, and retrieval.
- Retrieval layer: curated source database, public documents, policy library, source metadata.
- Audit layer: immutable event logs for AI prompts, outputs, model versions, source references, human approvals, and corrections.
- Deployment: EU-based infrastructure; Swedish/EU data protection posture.

### 9.2 Data Separation

At minimum, separate:

1. identity data;
2. authentication/session data;
3. deliberation contributions;
4. votes and preference signals;
5. AI interaction logs;
6. moderation and incident records;
7. research-consented datasets;
8. public transparency outputs.

### 9.3 Audit Logging Requirements

Log:

- agent name and version;
- model/provider/version where possible;
- prompt template version;
- retrieval sources used;
- user-visible output;
- confidence/uncertainty markers;
- human approval status;
- corrections;
- appeals;
- incidents;
- constitutional checks.

Do not publicly expose:

- private user identity;
- private deliberation content outside consent terms;
- raw sensitive political data;
- security-sensitive exploit details;
- personal data in prompts or logs unless necessary and legally justified.

## 10. Research Program

### 10.1 Core Research Questions

1. Can AI-assisted deliberation improve participants' perceived understanding of complex policy issues?
2. Does source-linked AI explanation increase or decrease trust?
3. Can AI summarization preserve minority positions accurately?
4. What failure modes occur when AI agents participate in political deliberation?
5. How do users respond to explicit uncertainty disclosures?
6. Does AI assistance reduce polarization or merely make arguments more sophisticated?
7. Can transparent audit logs create legitimacy, or do they overload participants?
8. How should human ratification be designed when AI generates most of the analytical work?
9. What governance mechanisms best prevent manipulation by humans, bots, foreign actors, or agent operators?
10. What level of autonomy is socially acceptable, legally defensible, and empirically safe?

### 10.2 Metrics

Participation:
- signups;
- active participants;
- return rate;
- deliberation depth;
- diversity of viewpoints where measurable and ethical.

Deliberation quality:
- argument diversity;
- evidence use;
- reduction in repeated claims;
- preservation of minority views;
- participant-rated fairness.

Trust and legitimacy:
- trust in AI summaries;
- trust in human governance;
- perceived transparency;
- perceived manipulation risk;
- willingness to accept outcomes.

AI performance:
- factual accuracy;
- citation quality;
- uncertainty calibration;
- correction rate;
- constitutional violation rate;
- hallucination rate;
- prompt-injection resilience.

Safety:
- incidents;
- escalations;
- manipulation attempts;
- moderation interventions;
- data protection issues;
- red-team findings and remediation time.

Policy output:
- number of proposals;
- number revised after evidence review;
- number with published trade-off analysis;
- number with minority reports;
- number reaching human ratification or lab signal.

## 11. Launch Phases

### Phase 0: Foundation

Deliverables:
- public project framing;
- white paper publication;
- draft AI Constitution;
- draft Member Conduct Framework;
- draft Research Ethics and Data Governance Policy;
- model/agent inventory;
- risk register;
- initial platform architecture.

Exit criteria:
- documents published;
- internal review complete;
- legal/privacy review initiated;
- no public claims of autonomous political authority.

### Phase 1: Closed Research Prototype

Participants:
- founding team;
- invited advisors;
- limited research participants.

Features:
- document Q&A;
- proposal drafting sandbox;
- deliberation summarization;
- audit logs;
- feedback forms.

Exit criteria:
- baseline safety evaluation;
- first red-team report;
- consent flow tested;
- correction mechanism tested.

### Phase 2: Public Lab Beta

Participants:
- public users with clear lab disclaimers;
- optional verified participants for deeper deliberation.

Features:
- public Q&A;
- topic-based deliberation;
- policy lab experiments;
- public transparency dashboard;
- published correction log.

Exit criteria:
- first public safety report;
- first research note;
- demonstrated handling of incidents and corrections;
- validated human review workflow.

### Phase 3: Formalization Readiness

Purpose:
- prepare for possible formal party or civic association structure.

Deliverables:
- bylaws draft;
- AI Constitution ratification process;
- member governance model;
- legal analysis;
- external audit;
- autonomy expansion criteria.

Exit criteria:
- explicit human decision about whether and how to formalize;
- public postmortem of lab phase;
- updated risk and governance documents.

## 12. Immediate Gaps and Clarifications in the White Paper

Correction after exporting the Google Doc as plain text: several sections that first appeared blank were present as tables in the original document. The timeline, financing table, risk table, and Appendix A do exist. The earlier extraction path through the Docs API flattened or skipped table content.

The English v2.2 document is strong as a vision document, but several operational sections still need expansion before platform launch:

1. The referenced five-step decision process still needs explicit step definitions suitable for implementation.
2. Timeline 2026 exists and includes April–December milestones, but should be converted into an executable launch plan with owners, dependencies, and exit criteria.
3. Risk Analysis and Countermeasures exists as a table, but should be expanded into a live risk register with severity scoring, owners, mitigations, residual risk, review cadence, and incident links.
4. Appendix A exists and lists frameworks/sources, but should be expanded with links, citations, and how each framework maps to OD governance.
5. The AI Constitution is described but not yet written as a complete governance document.
6. The Member Conduct Framework is referenced but not yet written.
7. The accountability matrix is promised but not yet specified.
8. Research ethics, informed consent, and publication rules are not yet detailed.
9. The platform architecture is directional but not implementation-ready.
10. The transition boundary between "research lab", "party in formation", and "formal party" needs sharper legal and public communication language.

## 14. Suggested Public Positioning

Short version:

"Oberoende Digital is building a Safe Autonomous Party Lab: a transparent environment for testing how AI agents can help humans deliberate, understand evidence, draft policy, and hold political organizations accountable. AI may assist, summarize, critique, and propose — but democratic legitimacy remains human, auditable, and contestable."

Longer version:

"We are not asking people to hand democracy over to machines. We are testing whether AI can make democratic work more transparent, evidence-based, and accountable when constrained by a public AI Constitution, human ratification, audit logs, independent review, and strong data protection. The goal is to learn in public before claiming authority."

## 15. First Build Recommendation

The first build should not be a full party platform. It should be a narrow, safe, demonstrable prototype:

"OD Lab v0.1: White Paper Explorer + Policy Deliberation Sandbox"

Features:

1. Public AI Q&A over the white paper with citations.
2. Clear AI-generated-content labeling.
3. Feedback button for incorrect or misleading answers.
4. Proposal sandbox where users can ask for policy drafts.
5. Evidence table generator.
6. Deliberation summary demo.
7. Public correction log.
8. Basic transparency page showing model, sources, limitations, and governance rules.
9. Consent-based research survey.
10. Admin review queue for outputs that may be published.

This gives OD something concrete to launch, test, and study without prematurely granting AI political authority.
