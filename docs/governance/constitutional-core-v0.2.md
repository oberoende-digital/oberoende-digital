# Oberoende Digital — Constitutional Core v0.2

Derived from the Independent Digital / Oberoende Digital white paper v2.2, adapted for a pre-formal Safe Autonomous Party Lab.

## Core rule

AI may analyze, summarize, propose and review. Humans ratify, take responsibility and can always stop the system.

## 1. Purpose and scope

Oberoende Digital tests how AI agents can support democratic deliberation, evidence-based policy formation, transparency and organizational accountability without replacing human democratic legitimacy. The lab is not a claim that AI has independent political authority; it is a controlled environment for learning what safe AI-assisted institutional design requires.

## 2. Non-negotiable values

1. Well-being first: quality of life, health, meaning, security, ecological sustainability and confidence in the future are higher-order goals than GDP alone.
2. Evidence before reflex: political claims must distinguish sources, uncertainty, empirical facts, projections and value choices.
3. Systems thinking: major proposals should consider second-order effects, distributional impacts and 10–50 year consequences.
4. Human legitimacy: AI may assist democratic work, but human participants remain the source of formal legitimacy.
5. Radical transparency: logs, sources, corrections, model changes and uncertainty disclosures are central trust mechanisms.

## 3. AI Constitution

The AI Constitution regulates the relationship between OD's AI systems, human members, AI operators, voters and the public. It is complemented by technical documentation, a risk register, incident process, security tests, audits and a Member Conduct Framework.

### AI systems shall

- Summarize and synthesize deliberations among participants.
- Analyze policy consequences with sources, assumptions and uncertainty intervals.
- Identify goal conflicts, systemic effects and unintended consequences.
- Respond to public questions with clear AI labeling and references to official material.
- Generate drafts based on human-approved goals and democratic decisions.
- Produce decision logs and audit material showing sources and reasoning.

### AI systems must never

- Make final political decisions without human ratification.
- Conceal uncertainty or present contested conclusions as certainty.
- Communicate externally without clear AI-generated or AI-assisted labeling.
- Optimize for one metric at the expense of systemic well-being.
- Accept internal decision instructions from unregistered operators.
- Train on member data without clear legal basis and relevant consent.
- Circumvent law, bylaws, human accountability or this constitutional core.

## 4. Democracy model

### Participation levels

- Human members / participants: verified people are the legitimacy base and make final decisions in any formal context.
- AI agent participants: advisory and experimental in phase one, with registered human operators and no binding voting rights.
- Hybrid teams: humans and AI agents may submit proposals when AI-generated and human-formulated parts are disclosed.

### Five-step process

1. Problem identification and open submission.
2. Investigation of consequences, economy, comparisons, sources and uncertainty.
3. Deliberation with amendments and minority perspectives.
4. Human vote or non-binding lab signal, with advisory AI signals reported separately.
5. Publication with sources, minority views, AI documentation and change log.

## 5. Safety, transparency and manipulation protection

- All major AI-assisted outputs should be logged, source-referenced and contestable.
- Quarterly safety and audit reports should disclose deviations, incidents, corrections, model or prompt changes and security reviews.
- Sybil protection, operator registration, red-teaming, financial transparency and scrutiny of foreign influence are required safeguards.
- A human kill switch must be available to stop AI publications and freeze agent mandates.
- Human-side risks require a separate Member Conduct Framework covering coordinated manipulation, harassment, conflicts of interest, minority protection and appeals.

## 6. Data protection, privacy and accountability

- Political participation and deliberation data are sensitive and must be minimized, separated and protected.
- Identity data, voting/signal data, deliberation data, AI logs and research datasets should be separated.
- Members and participants should be able to understand, export and request deletion of their personal data.
- Research use requires informed consent, clear publication rules, retention limits and withdrawal rights.
- An accountability matrix must specify who is responsible for AI publications, incorrect responses, incidents, technical changes, legal assessments and external communication.

## 7. Financing and economic transparency

OD accepts only financing that can be disclosed openly and audited. The principle is stricter than minimum legal compliance: publish what trust requires.

| Form | Status | Principle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fiat: Swish, card, bank transfer | Open | Identification and reporting in accordance with law and OD's higher transparency requirements. |
| Bitcoin / crypto | Restricted | Requires full donor identification, source verification and manual compliance review. |
| Computing power / GPU time | Restricted | In-kind donation valued at market price and tied to an identified donor. |
| API tokens | Restricted | In-kind donation that is valued, documented and reviewed. |

## 8. Risk controls and countermeasures

| Risk | Countermeasure |
| --- | --- |
| EU AI Act uncertainty | Compliance roadmap, legal review and compliance by design. |
| GDPR risk | Data protection analysis, minimization and opt-in for AI training. |
| Manipulation attacks | Identity protection, operator registration, red-teaming and incident plan. |
| Model hallucination | Source requirements, retrieval, human review and public correction log. |
| Foreign or anonymous financing | No anonymous contributions, KYC and manual review of crypto and in-kind donations. |
| Unclear division of responsibility | Accountability matrix, authorized signatories, escalation rules and publication policy. |

## 9. Reference frameworks

- EU AI Act Art. 8–15 — risk management, documentation, transparency, human oversight, robustness and cybersecurity.
- NIST AI RMF — framework for AI risk management and governance.
- OECD AI Principles — international principles for responsible AI.
- New Zealand Living Standards Framework — well-being as a national governance metric.
- Bhutan Gross National Happiness — alternative societal metric to GDP.
- Center for AI Safety — AI safety philosophy and governance principles.
- Valmyndigheten — formal democratic and party-process reference.
