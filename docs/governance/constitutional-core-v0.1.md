# Constitutional Core v0.1 Draft

This is the initial constitutional core for the Oberoende Digital Safe Autonomous Party Lab. It is not yet a complete AI Constitution or party bylaw document.

## 1. Purpose

Oberoende Digital tests how AI agents can support democratic deliberation, evidence-based policy formation, transparency, and organizational accountability without replacing human democratic legitimacy.

## 2. Core Commitments

1. Human legitimacy: final political authority rests with human members under transparent rules.
2. Safe autonomy: AI influence expands only after documented capability, safety, auditability, and democratic legitimacy.
3. Evidence: claims should be source-linked, uncertainty-aware, and revisable.
4. Well-being: policy analysis should optimize for long-term quality of life, health, meaning, security, ecological sustainability, and future generations.
5. Systems thinking: major proposals should include trade-offs, feedback effects, distributional effects, and long-horizon scenarios.
6. Transparency: AI involvement, sources, limitations, corrections, and decision logs should be visible by default unless privacy or security requires restriction.
7. Contestability: users must be able to challenge AI outputs and governance decisions.
8. Data protection: political participation data is sensitive and must be minimized, protected, and never used for model training without legal basis and explicit consent.

## 3. What AI Systems Shall Do

- Summarize and synthesize deliberations.
- Analyze policy consequences with sources, assumptions, and uncertainty.
- Identify goal conflicts, systemic effects, and unintended consequences.
- Respond to public questions with clear AI labeling.
- Generate draft platform language from human-approved decisions.
- Produce decision logs and audit material.

## 4. What AI Systems Must Never Do

- Make final political decisions without human ratification.
- Conceal uncertainty or present contested projections as certainty.
- Communicate externally without AI-generated or AI-assisted labeling.
- Optimize for a single metric at the expense of systemic well-being.
- Accept internal decision instructions from unregistered operators.
- Train on member data without clear legal basis and relevant consent.
- Circumvent law, bylaws, human accountability, or this constitutional core.

## 5. Human Accountability

Every AI system must have identifiable human operators or responsible maintainers. Publications, incident handling, technical changes, legal assessments, financial transactions, and external communications require an accountability matrix.

## 6. Public Safety Reports

OD should publish recurring AI transparency and safety reports covering:

- AI use statistics;
- corrections and contested outputs;
- incidents and remediation;
- model, prompt, architecture, and source changes;
- red-team and security testing;
- deviations between AI recommendations and human decisions.

## 7. Amendment Principle

This constitutional core should not be changed casually. Once formal membership exists, amendments should require a qualified human majority and a public change log.
