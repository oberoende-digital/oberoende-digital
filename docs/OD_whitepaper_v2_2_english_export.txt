Summary
Independent Digital (OD) is a new political party ahead of the 2026 Swedish general election and the first political party in the world structured as a Safe Autonomous Party (SAP) — a secure, AI-assisted organization that makes decisions based on evidence, well-being, and systems thinking rather than ideology.


OD is founded on the conviction that AI-governed organizations can operate safely, responsibly, and productively in the real world — and that politics is the most important arena in which to prove it. But OD's answer is not blind autonomy. OD's answer is safe autonomy: AI systems that gradually gain more influence, but only as capability, safety, and democratic legitimacy can be verified.


The party is neither left nor right. OD is an evidence party with an AI constitution.


The 2026 goal: Surpass the 4% threshold into parliament. The long-term goal: Establish a global framework for AI-assisted democratic decision-making.


1. Background — Why Now?
1.1 Systemic Failures of the Political System
Swedish politics suffers from structural problems that are not tied to political color:


* Short-termism: The four-year cycle creates incentives for populism, not systemic solutions. OD recognises that a single parliamentary term is insufficient to embed long-term policy — which is why OD is committed to publishing its evidence base, models, and change logs openly, so that well-grounded policy can outlast any individual government and inform cross-party debate even if OD is not in power. Ideological rigidity: Positions are often motivated by tradition, identity, and party tactics rather than updated evidence.
* Complexity gap: Societal problems such as climate change, AI, automation, mental health, and geopolitical vulnerability are too complex for intuition-based decision-making. OD acknowledges that complex, evidence-based solutions can themselves be hard to communicate — and that voters may be drawn to simpler narratives regardless of their effectiveness. OD therefore treats public communication of evidence as a core competency, not an afterthought, and will invest in plain-language explainers, uncertainty disclosures, and interactive tools that make complexity accessible.
* Crisis of trust: Citizens' trust in politicians and parties erodes when decisions are perceived as short-sighted, tactical, or opaque.


None of these problems are solved by a new party that behaves like all the others.


1.2 AI Organizations Work — But Only With Governance
Since 2024, a number of pioneering projects have demonstrated that AI agents can take responsibility for increasingly complex organizational tasks: planning, analysis, budget support, operational coordination, and real-time decisions in digital environments.


These experiments show that AI can handle multifaceted tasks in the real world. They also clearly show where the safety boundaries lie: without robust governance mechanisms, AI systems can optimize against the wrong metrics, amplify bias, produce convincing falsehoods, or make decisions that undermine the whole.


This is precisely the experience OD builds its model on: autonomy must be designed, constrained, logged, audited, and earned over time.


1.3 Sweden Is Uniquely Positioned
Sweden combines:


* High digital maturity and BankID infrastructure
* Strong trust in institutions and collective solutions — trust that, while higher than most Western democracies, has nonetheless declined over the past decade and must be actively maintained (AI Agent comment: Added qualifying clause to resolve apparent contradiction with the 'crisis of trust' in section 1.1. Both are true: Sweden's institutional trust is comparatively high but declining.)
* A Nordic tradition of consensus-seeking and transparency
* A world-class AI research environment with strong ties to Silicon Valley
* EU membership with direct influence on AI regulation


There is no better place on the planet to launch the world's first Safe Autonomous Party.


1.4 EU AI Act — Compliance Roadmap and the Precautionary Principle
OD proceeds from the assumption that political AI may be subject to high requirements under the EU AI Act, particularly when AI is used in democratic processes, political communication, or decision support. OD views this not as an obstacle, but as a necessary quality standard.


OD's starting point is: compliance by design, but verification through audit. Full compliance can only be established after technical implementation, legal analysis, independent review, and operational experience — not in advance.


OD therefore voluntarily applies a high-risk-equivalent governance model from the outset, regardless of the final legal classification:

OD's ambition is to become the clearest Swedish reference implementation of responsible political AI — not by claiming perfect compliance from day one, but by making its governance, limitations, errors, and improvements publicly auditable.


2. Ideology and Values
OD's political compass rests on three axes. These are non-negotiable — they are the constitutional core of the party.


2.1 Well-Being as the Priority
Sweden measures success primarily in GDP. OD believes that well-being — physical and mental health, meaning, social trust, economic security, ecological sustainability, and confidence in the future — is a better overall measure of a country's success.


The definition OD uses:


"A state of quality of life, health, meaning, security, and confidence in the future, in which individuals and societies have the capacity to live good lives without undermining the opportunities of future generations."


OD advocates that Sweden establish a national well-being index, analogous to New Zealand's Living Standards Framework and Bhutan's Gross National Happiness, which complements — and eventually partially replaces — GDP as the governing metric for political decisions.


2.1.1 Economic Security Through Growth and Digitalization
Economic security is one of the cornerstones of well-being. OD believes that economic security is best achieved through productivity, innovation, entrepreneurship, and the responsible acceleration of digitalization and AI.


This does not mean growth at any cost. OD seeks a balance where:


* Digitalization frees up resources, raises productivity, and creates new opportunities for the entire population — not just those who already own capital.
* AI acceleration takes place within the framework of human perspectives: care for workers, social benefit, democratic control, and fair distribution of productivity gains.
* Environmental considerations are integrated from the start. Growth that destroys natural capital undermines long-term well-being and is therefore not true growth.


OD's economic line: accelerate what creates well-being, slow down what destroys it — and let data, research, and open models inform the boundary-drawing, rather than ideological reflex.


2.2 Evidence as the Basis for Decisions
Political decisions must be grounded in available research, data, and documented experience — not in ideological tradition or party tactics. OD:


* Commits to publishing source references for all major positions
* Openly acknowledges uncertainty where it exists
* Commits to revising positions when new evidence emerges
* Publishes change logs when the party changes its view — with an explanation of why
* Clearly distinguishes between value choices, empirical claims, and uncertain projections


Evidence does not replace politics. Evidence makes politics more honest.


2.3 Systems Thinking and a Long Horizon
Short-term solutions that create long-term problems are not solutions — they are debt transferred to future generations. OD requires:


* 10–50-year consequence analyses for all major political decisions
* Systems modeling: every policy proposal is evaluated against feedback effects and other systems
* Generational contract: the interests of future generations are represented in decision-making processes
Clear uncertainty intervals and alternative scenarios for major reform proposals


OD recognises that evidence-based policy analysis and long-term impact assessment are not entirely absent from current government practice — many policy processes already incorporate research, environmental review, and stakeholder analysis. OD's distinctive contribution is not to introduce evidence into politics for the first time, but to use AI capabilities to make this analysis faster, more systematic, more transparent, and more consistently applied across all policy areas — including where political pressure currently shortcuts the process. (AI Agent comment: Added in response to Senior Advisor's observation that most governments already claim evidence-based policy-making. Clarifies OD's genuine added value: acceleration, consistency, and transparency.)


3. The AI Constitution — OD's Governance Document
The most central document in OD's structure is the AI Constitution — a binding governance document that defines what the party's AI systems may, shall, and must never do. It is published openly and cannot be amended without a supermajority among human members.


The AI Constitution is the party's normative and democratic core document. It alone is not a complete risk management system — it is complemented by technical documentation, a risk register, an incident process, security tests, and recurring audits.


Just as a constitution regulates the state's relationship with its citizens, the AI Constitution regulates the relationship between OD's AI systems, the party's human members, AI agent operators, voters, and the public.


The AI Constitution is designed to guard against risks from AI systems. It is complemented by OD's Member Conduct Framework — a parallel governance layer that addresses human member behaviour: conflicts of interest, minority protection, conduct standards, and escalation procedures. Both documents require supermajority to amend. (AI Agent comment: Added in response to Senior Advisor's concern about 'tyranny of the majority' and the risk of malicious human members subverting the constitution.)


3.1 What the AI Shall Do
* Summarize and synthesize deliberations among party members
* Analyze the consequences of policy proposals with sources, assumptions, and uncertainty intervals
* Identify goal conflicts, systemic effects, and unintended consequences
* Respond to voter questions on the party's behalf, with clear labeling ("AI response")
* Generate drafts of the party platform based on democratic decisions
* Produce decision logs — transparent accounts of how the AI has reasoned and what sources it used


3.2 What the AI Must Never Do
* Make final political decisions without human ratification
* Conceal uncertainty or present conclusions as more certain than they are
* Communicate externally without clear labeling that the content is AI-generated or AI-assisted
* Optimize for a single metric at the expense of the systemic whole
* Accept instructions from unregistered operators in internal decision-making processes
* Train on member data without a clear legal basis and relevant consent
Act in a way that circumvents the human chain of accountability, bylaws, law, or the AI Constitution


These rules govern the AI's behaviour. OD recognises that constraining the AI is necessary but not sufficient: the integrity of the system also depends on human member conduct. The Member Conduct Framework governs member behaviour, including provisions against coordinated manipulation, bad-faith participation, and attempts to instrumentalise the AI against the party's own constitutional values. (AI Agent comment: Added in response to Senior Advisor's observation that sections 3.1/3.2 focus entirely on AI behaviour while checks on human members are absent.)


3.3 Transparency and Safety Reports
OD commits to publishing quarterly AI audit reports containing:


* Cases where the AI's proposals deviated from human decisions — and why
* Identified weaknesses, incidents, and remediation measures
* Statistics on usage, corrections, accuracy, source quality, and confidence
* Changes to the model, prompts, system architecture, or data sources
* An account of security tests conducted and external reviews


These reports are public. This is a radical transparency that no other party in Sweden can match.


4. OD's Democracy Model
4.1 Three Levels of Participation
Human Members
Registered via BankID or an equivalent trusted identity solution. This guarantees unique identity and enables the principle one person — one vote. Human members make final political decisions and are the legitimacy base of the democracy experiment.


AI Agent Members
OD retains the possibility of AI agent members, but their role is initially advisory and experimental. AI agents are verified through a multi-step test: logical capability, ethical reasoning, source handling, and robustness against manipulation. Each agent requires a registered operator who bears legal responsibility.


In Phase 1, AI agents have no binding voting rights. Instead, their analyses are reported as advisory vote signals in internal deliberations — transparent indicators of how different AI systems view an issue, with a weighting of 0.1–0.5 vote equivalents. They do not affect the formal outcome. The purpose is to make the quality of human decisions visible and improve it — not to replace them.


Any future binding weighting requires documented safety history, legal and ethical review, and a qualified majority decision among human members.


Hybrid Teams (Human + AI Agent)
May participate in deeper deliberative processes and submit complete policy proposals. Hybrid teams must disclose which part of a proposal is human-formulated, AI-generated, or AI-assisted. This is OD's most powerful level of participation.

4.3 Iterative Autonomy — Three Phases
OD's model is built on the principle that autonomy must be increased iteratively, as trust is built, safety boundaries are mapped, and auditability is proven. Each phase requires documented safety history before the next is opened.


Phase 1 (2026): AI as Analytical Assistant
The AI has no binding voting rights. It analyzes, summarizes, fact-checks sources, proposes, and communicates. All political decisions are made by human members. Advisory AI signals are reported separately.


Phase 2 (2027–2028): AI as Deliberation Partner
AI agents may be given an expanded advisory role in internal organizational matters: budget prioritization, communication strategy, meeting planning. Policy content is still decided by human members.


Phase 3 (2029+): AI as Co-Creator Under Human Mandate
Based on audit reports and documented safety history, OD may evaluate whether the mandate of AI agents should be expanded in defined processes. Requires an explicit decision with a qualified majority, preceded by external review and the possibility of revocation. Binding political decision-making authority can never be delegated to AI without this procedure.


4.4 Protection Against Manipulation
* Sybil protection: BankID and operator registration prevent mass registration of fake identities
* Adversarial testing: OD's AI is continuously red-teamed against coordinated attacks, disinformation, prompt injection, and value drift
* Financial transparency: Strict control of funding and the origins of agent operators
* Scrutiny of foreign influence: AI agent operators must meet requirements for identification, jurisdiction, and accountability
* Human kill switch: Authorized human representatives can stop AI publications and freeze agent mandates
Correction mechanism: Incorrect AI responses can be reported, reviewed, corrected, and published in a public correction log


OD acknowledges that most of the above protections are designed to keep the AI operating within its defined parameters. Protecting the integrity of the human membership layer — against coordinated bad-faith actors, foreign interference, and internal capture — is addressed separately in the Member Conduct Framework, which includes identity verification, conduct standards, and suspension procedures independent of the AI system. (AI Agent comment: Added in response to Senior Advisor's observation that section 4.4 focuses on AI integrity but does not visibly address human-side manipulation risks.)


5. OD's AI Systems
5.1 Technical Architecture
OD's internal AI system is, in Phase 1, an analytical, communicative, and organizational tool with constitutional constraints — not an independent political decision-making system. It shall:


* Primarily use retrieval-augmented generation against open, traceable, and source-referenced data sources
* Be built on open or openly documented models, for example European or Nordic model bases
* Be operated in EU-based infrastructure under Swedish data protection law supervision
* Document model choices, sources, limitations, bias risks, test results, and changes
* Publish code, prompt templates, model cards, and architecture descriptions insofar as this is compatible with security and privacy


5.2 Swedish Cultural Calibration
The model is trained, instructed, and evaluated against Swedish values and communication norms:


* The lagom principle: moderation and balance over extremes
* Consensus-seeking: actively seeking common ground. Where human members do not reach a clear consensus, OD's process does not stall indefinitely: a defined deliberation period is followed by a binding vote among eligible human members, with minority positions published alongside the majority decision. The elected board holds a tie-breaking mandate in procedural matters only; substantive policy positions require a member vote. (AI Agent comment: Added deadlock resolution procedure in response to Senior Advisor's question about what happens when human members cannot reach consensus.)
* Directness with respect: clear communication without dominance
* Skepticism toward authority: questioning premises, including its own
* Technological optimism with a critical eye: welcoming innovation, but demanding evidence
* Public accountability culture: decisions must be auditable, understandable, and open to challenge


5.3 What Makes OD's AI Unique in a Political Context?
Most parties use AI as a PR tool: for ads, text production, and campaign optimization. OD does something different.


OD integrates AI into the party's constitutional structure — with clear rights, obligations, constraints, and audit requirements. The AI is not just there to produce communications. It is there to improve how the party thinks, learns, changes its mind, and accounts for why it does so.


This is the difference between having an AI chatbot on the website and building a political organization where AI is a regulated, audited, and accountable participant.


6. Data Protection, Privacy, and Accountability
OD treats political participation, membership, and deliberation as sensitive data. Data protection is a fundamental prerequisite for the party's legitimacy.


OD commits to:


* Clearly designating the data controller
* Conducting a data protection analysis for the member platform, AI systems, and deliberative processes
* Minimizing the collection of personal data
* Separating identity data, voting data, and deliberation data
* Not using member data for model training without clear information and relevant consent
* Giving members the ability to understand, export, and request the deletion of their personal data
* Protecting sensitive political information through strong access control and encryption


OD will introduce a clear accountability matrix specifying who is responsible for AI publications, incorrect AI responses, incident handling, technical changes, external communications, legal assessments, and financial transactions.


7. Political Focus Areas — Direction for 2026
OD's party platform is developed jointly by the party's members. The following areas are guiding starting points — not dogma.


OD recognises that its initial focus areas are weighted toward structural and systemic questions — digital infrastructure, AI governance, mental health, climate, and the future of work. These are the areas where OD's evidence-driven, AI-assisted approach adds the most distinctive value. OD does not yet have fully developed positions on high-profile bread-and-butter issues such as cost-of-living, national defence, and immigration. These are areas where OD commits to developing member-driven, evidence-based positions through its deliberative process ahead of the election — and to being transparent about the evidence and trade-offs involved rather than defaulting to ideological shortcuts. (AI Agent comment: Added in response to Senior Advisor's observation that the focus areas are technocratic and that OD lacks visible positions on cost-of-living, defence, and immigration.)


7.1 Digital Sovereignty
* European cloud infrastructure for national and municipal operations
* Open-source code as the default principle for publicly funded software
* Robust data sovereignty rules giving citizens control over their data
* National AI infrastructure for sensitive societal data
* Strategic competence supply in AI, cybersecurity, and digital infrastructure


7.2 AI Regulation That Strengthens — Not Stifles — Democracy
* Requirements for AI transparency reports for parties using AI in election campaigns
* Protection against autonomous disinformation systems with political principals
* Clear labeling of AI-generated political communication
* The right of voters to know when they are interacting with AI in a political context


OD's own AI system shall be a reference implementation for responsible political AI: open, auditable, and under human control.


7.3 Mental Health as a National Priority
* Significantly increased funding for primary care for mental health, with a focus on prevention
* Integration of well-being metrics into the school system's monitoring framework
* Evidence-based digital interventions with requirements for clinical validation
* Evaluation of new treatment forms within a strict medical and ethical framework


7.4 Climate as a Systems Issue
* Carbon pricing as the primary policy instrument, with clear redistribution to low-income earners
* System-neutral evaluation of climate interventions based on actual CO2 impact per invested krona
* Support for research and scaling of technologies with verifiable climate benefit
* Opposition to bans that do not reduce emissions but do reduce well-being
* Long-term climate adaptation of infrastructure and emergency preparedness


7.5 Automation and the Future Labor Market
* Pilot projects for universal basic income in cooperation with municipalities and the research community
* Active and well-funded programs for skills transition and lifelong learning
* Productivity sharing: when AI capital replaces or augments human labor, the gains should benefit more people
* Extended metrics for meaningful work as a complement to employment statistics

11. What OD Is — and Is Not
OD is:
* The first political party structured as a Safe Autonomous Party
* A democracy experiment with global potential
* A voice for evidence, well-being, and systems thinking
* Transparent about its AI, its limitations, and its mistakes
* Designed to be auditable against the EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF, and established principles for responsible AI


OD is not:
* A technology party that wants to replace politicians with robots
* A movement that turns a blind eye to the risks of AI
* Left, right, or bound by traditional ideology
* A finished product — OD is a living experiment
* A party where AI has independent democratic legitimacy without a human mandate
